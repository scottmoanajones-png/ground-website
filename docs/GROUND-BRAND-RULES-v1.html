01

What Ground is

Ground is the money infrastructure company. Its API connects any stablecoin or cash balance on a financial platform to the full on-chain yield grid — 24+ sources, noncustodial, institutional-grade. Integration is measured in days.

The ambition: be to on-chain yield what Stripe is to payment processing. The default, invisible, trusted layer that serious financial platforms rely on.

Audience

Strictly B2B. Four decision-makers must each feel understood at every touchpoint:

  • CEO / Founder Strategic fit, category leadership, investor credibility
  • CFO / CIO Financial authority, risk posture, yield numbers
  • General Manager Implementation path, integration simplicity
  • Senior Engineer API quality, noncustodial architecture, uptime

Primary verticals

Neobanks · Fintechs · Centralized and decentralized exchanges · Custodians · Wallets · Corporate treasuries

02

Core metaphor Locked Apr 30 2026

In electronics, ground is the stable reference layer every circuit must connect to. A floating voltage source has potential but no reference point — it can't be measured, can't deliver current, can't do work. The moment you connect it to ground, the reference is established. Current flows. The energy was always there. Ground is what lets it act.

Floating — Idle capital. No reference. Potential is theoretical. Yield is zero.
Grounded — Connected. Reference established. Circuit complete. Yield is live.

This maps directly to the product. Idle stablecoin and cash balances are floating — they have potential but no reference. Connecting to Ground establishes the reference. Every yield source on the on-chain grid becomes accessible.

The name IS the metaphor. The logo (three tapered bars, 3:2:1 ratio) is the electrical ground symbol ⏚. Already inscribed. This metaphor generates copy naturally — plug in, floating, current, the grid, live, circuit complete — and reads in 3 seconds to a CFO with no prior crypto knowledge.

Vocabulary it unlocks

grounded · floating · connected · live · the grid · current · reference · circuit complete · on-chain · activate · plug in

Vocabulary it replaces

supercharges → connects, grounds, activates
The previous verb implied adding energy from outside. Ground's product unleashes potential that was already there.

Visual direction Locked May 7 2026

After client alignment on May 7, 2026: the grid is the infrastructure play, and the top-down to isometric perspective shift is the brand's core visual move.

The idea in one sentence: Imagery is built from grids. The viewer sees this imagery either top-down or isometric. Shifting perspective reveals new angles and new meaning — the same underlying infrastructure, suddenly made tangible.

What it means

  • Top-down Infrastructure seen from above. A schematic. A plan. Precision and control. The "map view" — familiar, legible, authoritative.
  • Isometric Infrastructure made visible and tangible. Depth and volume revealed. The same grid, now with height — blocks rise to show activity, yield, growth.
  • The shift Moving between views is Ground's signature moment. The camera lift that reveals what was always there. Same infrastructure, new perspective, new meaning.

What came before (retired)

Cymatics / Flatlands (Edwin Abbott) was the previous visual direction. Retired April 30, 2026. Visually sophisticated but required explanation, disconnected from the name, didn't generate copy naturally. Do not reference or revive it.

05

The grid system

The grid is not decoration. It is the fundamental visual unit of the brand — it represents infrastructure literally and metaphorically. Every canvas, illustration, and data visualization is built on the grid.

Construction rules

  • Start top-down Every canvas defaults to the plan view. Top-down is the base state — infrastructure as schematic.
  • Reveal isometric Isometric is the transition, the reveal, the "aha." It shows volume and meaning, not just structure.
  • Grid lines Thin, structural, quiet. Never heavy. Never decorative. The grid should feel like engineering drawing paper, not graph paper wallpaper.
  • Cube faces Top face: lightest (lit). Left face: mid-tone. Right face: darkest (shadow). Consistent light source — upper-left.
  • Height = meaning Block height encodes data — yield value, activity level, connectivity. Taller = more. Empty grid = floating/idle.
  • Signal range = active Renderer activity lives in the shared signal family: Signal Deep, Signal Mid, Signal Bright, and Glow Soft. Brand green remains the primary interface accent; the signal range is for motion, depth, and active grid behavior.
  • Projection angle Standard isometric: 30° from horizontal. X/Y/Z ratios consistent across all scenes. Do not freehand the angle.

Named scenes

Defrag
Scattered blocks assemble into ordered structure. Chaos → order. Floating → grounded.
Crystal
Growth from a seed point outward. A single green cube propagates. Yield activating.
Route
A path traces across the grid — green lighting up as the circuit connects. Current flowing.
Terrain
A yield landscape — block heights encode yield values across sources. The full grid at work.

Do not

Don't use the grid as wallpaper or backdrop texture. Don't animate it gratuitously. Every grid canvas should earn its place — it should tell something about the product or represent something real about infrastructure, yield, or connectivity.

Usage rules

  • Paper ramp Paper, Paper Soft, and Paper Strong are the default light surfaces. They should feel cooler and more infrastructural than parchment or notebook stock.
  • Ink / Hero Dark Ink is the primitive dark base. Hero Dark is now a semantic alias, not a separate color. Dark Surface is the next tonal step for occupied cells, panels, and layered dark UI.
  • Brand green Yield and Yield Bright are still the primary brand accents: links, CTA emphasis, selected interface moments, and restrained headline highlights. One green thing at a time.
  • Animation palette All renderer scenes share one signal family: Signal Deep, Signal Mid, Signal Bright, and Glow Soft. Do not recreate separate about/platform/stream palettes.
  • Semantic status Success, Warning, Error, and Info are official operational colors. They are for product and system state, not for general marketing decoration.

What is prohibited

No neon. No rainbow palette drift. No inventing new scene-specific colors when the shared system already covers the job. No gradient text. No using semantic status hues as casual accents. Dark remains a deliberate section or surface choice, not the brand's resting state.

Typography

One family: IBM Plex. Three variants. This is non-negotiable — the brand does not mix in display fonts, system fonts, or novelty typefaces. The system is now role-based: 21px for reading, 16px for compact UI/legal, and the rest of the scale as named display steps.

Hard rules

  • Loaded weights Serif 400 only. Sans 400 and 500. Mono 400 and 500. Do not introduce heavier weights or extra cuts without an intentional system revision.
  • Official type set 12 / 16 / 21 / 28 / 37 / 50 / 67 / 89 / 118. Name by role, not by HTML tag: Label XS, UI Base, Reading Base, Title S, Title M, Section, Hero, Display XL, Display XXL.
  • Reading base 21px is the default prose size. Ground is a reading-first website, so body copy should feel generous, deliberate, and confident.
  • UI base 16px is the compact UI/legal base. Use it for forms, nav, utility text, and places where 21px would feel bloated.
  • Serif role Hero display, section headings, card titles, and editorial emphasis. Not for body copy.
  • Sans role All running body copy, reading-first paragraphs, support copy, forms, and dense interface text.
  • Mono role Section eyebrows, nav, button labels, tags, chips, metadata, code references, and compact operational labels. Usually uppercase with tracking.
  • Semantic tags Do not tie `h1`/`h2`/`h3` directly to size tokens. Use tags for document structure, then map them onto the appropriate role-based type size.
  • Gradient text Never. Not even on dark backgrounds.
  • Tracking (letter-spacing) Mono labels: roughly 0.08–0.18em depending on density. Eyebrow caps: 0.12–0.18em. Hero serif can run tight at around -0.04em. Do not over-track sans body copy.
08

Logo

  • Mark Three tapered horizontal bars at 3:2:1 width ratio, stacked with narrowest at bottom. This IS the electrical ground symbol ⏚.
  • Wordmark GROUND in IBM Plex Sans, spaced uppercase. Paired with the mark to the right.
  • Viewbox 1151 × 240px. The SVG source file is in Assets/Ground-Logo.svg.
  • Colors On light: fill="black" (--ink). On dark: fill="white". Never color the mark in yield green or other accent colors.
  • Clear space Minimum clear space = height of the shortest bar on all sides.

Never

Distort the 3:2:1 ratio. Add drop shadows, glows, or gradients. Animate the logo mark. Use the mark without the wordmark except in very small / favicon contexts. Recolor in anything other than black or white.

The 3:2:1 ratio as a compositional principle

The bar ratio is not just the logo — it is a layout proportioning principle. Use it in grids, card ratios, and section proportions to build visual coherence across all brand surfaces.

09

Tone of voice

If Ground were a person at a dinner table: stoic yet ambitious, principled, buttoned up but lowkey, speaks with conviction, professional and coherent, has fun but well-behaved, aura of excitement, quiet confidence underlaid with competence.

Write like this

  • Declarative Short, confident sentences. "Integration is measured in days." Not "our platform enables you to integrate in just days."
  • Precise Specific numbers over vague superlatives. "24+ yield sources" over "dozens of integrations."
  • Mature Infrastructure confidence. Not startup enthusiasm. Assume the reader is a CFO who has seen every pitch.
  • Honest Don't hype. Don't promise what isn't proven. Let the stats and the product speak.

Reference brands for tone

Column.com · Stripe · Mercury · AWS · Linear.app
Not: Coinbase, Binance, any consumer DeFi brand

Preferred verbs

connects · grounds · activates · enables · produces · delivers · routes

Avoid

supercharges · unlocks · seamless · leverage · game-changing · revolutionary · best-in-class · world-class

Do / Don't

Do
  • Cool mineral paper (#ecf1f0) as the default background
  • IBM Plex family — serif, sans, mono only
  • 21px reading base, 16px compact UI base
  • Brand green as the primary accent, used sparingly
  • Semantic amber / red / blue reserved for status use
  • Grid-based layouts and compositions
  • Serif for headlines, mono for labels
  • One shared animation palette across all renderers
  • Top-down and isometric as the two permitted grid states
  • The ⏚ symbol as a repeatable brand element
  • Floating vs grounded as the visual grammar for product diagrams
  • Precise numbers over vague claims
  • Generous whitespace — let the grid breathe
  • Dark surfaces as deliberate hero, canvas, and CTA moments
Don't
  • Dark mode as the default / primary brand state
  • Neon, rainbow, or multi-color palette drift
  • Gradient text — ever
  • Separate about / platform / stream color systems
  • Using status colors as general marketing accents
  • Blob illustration or character art
  • Heavy blur, glossy glass theatrics, or generic SaaS sheen
  • Side-stripe accent borders
  • Cymatics / Flatlands visual language (retired Apr 30)
  • Anamorphic dot clouds or organic fluid shapes
  • The word "supercharges" or any hyperbole verb
  • Consumer or lifestyle aesthetic signals
  • Grid as decoration — every canvas must mean something
